# EDA Interview Exercise: Online Retail II Dataset

We would like you to perform an exploratory data analysis on a public online retail transaction dataset. The goal is not to build a perfect model or produce a polished consulting deck. The goal is to understand how you approach unfamiliar data, use analytical judgment, communicate findings, and use GenAI tools thoughtfully.

## Dataset

You will use the **Online Retail II** dataset, a public transaction-level dataset from a UK-based online retailer. Each row represents a transaction line item. The data includes fields such as invoice number, stock code, product description, quantity, invoice date, unit price, customer ID, and country.

**Data source:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mashlyn/online-retail-ii-uci

## Time expectation

Please spend no more than **2–3 hours** on this exercise.

## Deliverable

Please submit a single notebook that reads like a short analytical presentation. It should include code, charts/tables, and written interpretation. Please include the following sections:

### 1. Executive summary
Briefly summarize the most important things you found.

### 2. Data overview
Explain what the data appears to contain, including the apparent grain of the data.

### 3. Data quality checks
Identify missing values, duplicates, unusual values, outliers, or other issues that could affect interpretation.

### 4. Exploratory analysis
Explore sales, products, customers, time trends, countries, or other dimensions you think are relevant.

### 5. Findings and hypotheses
Present 3–5 findings or hypotheses. For each, explain why it matters and how confident you are.

### 6. Caveats
Explain what could be misleading, uncertain, or incomplete about the analysis.

### 7. Recommended next steps
If this were a real client project, what would you do next?

### 8. Use of GenAI
Briefly describe how you used GenAI tools, what you accepted or modified, and how you checked the results.

## Guidance

You may use Python, R, SQL, Databricks, notebooks, GenAI tools, documentation, or other resources. We expect you to use modern tools, including GenAI, where helpful. However, you are responsible for the correctness and interpretation of your final work.

Please do not try to analyze everything. We are more interested in a thoughtful, well-explained analysis than in a large number of charts.

## What we will discuss in the interview

During the interview, we will ask you to walk us through your notebook and discuss:

- What you checked first and why
- Which findings you trust most
- Which findings you are least certain about
- What data quality issues matter most
- How GenAI helped you
- How you validated or corrected GenAI output
- What you would do next on a real project
